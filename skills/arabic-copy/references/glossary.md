# Glossary and register

Terms and register choices that held up on shipped Gulf SaaS copy. Start each new product with a
copy of the "fixed terms" table and add its own nouns to it. A term chosen once is never varied.

## Register in one table

| Choice | Use | Not |
|---|---|---|
| Level | simple modern standard Arabic (فصحى بسيطة) | classical, or dialect |
| Test | would a Saudi professional in the audience write it to their manager? | anything more formal than that |
| Product UI and in-product assistant replies | فصحى, like the rest of the product | dialect |
| Chat with the person you work for | their language and register | — |
| Address, someone trying the product | singular: «جرّب»، «بياناتك» | mixing with plural on one page |
| Address, a company (contact, enterprise, legal) | formal plural: «لديكم»، «بياناتكم» | «الخاص بكم» |
| Body length | 15 to 25 words, two bullets a block | long paragraphs (except a direct answer to a direct question) |
| Promise words on a control or security page | what is refused, counted, recorded | «بسهولة»، «بذكاء» |

## Everyday words over literary ones

Pick the word the reader uses at work. If a word sounds like a speech or a poem, it is the wrong
one: «ارتقى» is rising in rank or morals, not growing in size («يكبر فريقك»).

## Terms that stay Latin

- Product, company and vendor names (the product itself, Slack, PDF readers, payment gateways).
- File formats and standards: PDF, XLSX, CSV, JSON, SSO, API.
- Engine and language names, SQL keywords, table and column names, hostnames, handles.
- Version numbers and identifiers.

Wrap a Latin run that sits next to punctuation in bidi isolates only after the rendered page shows
it flipping.

## Words an audience says as they say them

Keep the loanword the audience actually uses at work when the Arabic one would sound like a
textbook. For engineers: «البرودكشن», SQL keywords, engine names. For finance teams the Arabic
terms are standard: use them.

## Localisation checks

- **The Saudi working week starts on Sunday** (the weekend is Friday and Saturday). "Lands on
  Sunday morning" already fits; "Monday morning" for "start of the week" does not.
- One digit set and one date format per site. Don't mix Arabic-Indic and Western digits.
- No full stop on headings and buttons in Arabic, whatever the English does. Unify the file.
- A CTA is a verb. A nudge or reassurance line goes under it, and only with real terms (trial
  length, no card needed): ask for them rather than invent them.

## Demo and sample content

- Company names in Latin.
- Project, role, team and label names translated.
- People's names local to the market.
- Avatar initials derived from the translated name, never hardcoded.
- No famous sample-data names, and never the vendor's own name in a customer list.
